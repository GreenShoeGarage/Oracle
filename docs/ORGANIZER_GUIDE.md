# ORACLE organizer guide

ORACLE · LARP Field Kit helps you prepare a shared story, assign characters, and run short interactions around physical props and people. Start with one complete adventure, then add the instruments your event needs. Players use their own accounts and approved characters.

This guide describes v1.0.0. See [release status](STATUS.md) for the exact deployed commit and remaining acceptance checks, and the [player guide](PLAYER_GUIDE.md) for instructions you can give participants.

## Run your first adventure

1. Sign in at [oracle.greenshoegarage.com](https://oracle.greenshoegarage.com). On **Your events**, choose **Choose a starter adventure**. If you already have events, expand **Join or organize another event**.
2. Choose Fantasy — **The Last Lantern**, Cyberpunk — **The Last Neighborhood Signal**, or Wasteland — **The Last Water Beacon**. Each creates a separate Draft with a complete clue sequence, private preparation notes, and two approved, unassigned characters.
3. In **Prepare adventure**, read **Organizer notes · private**. Select **Print prop labels** and prepare the ordinary objects, envelope, paper puzzle, and meeting place described in those notes. Printouts contain prop identities, not the solutions.
4. For a practice run, choose **Make rehearsal copy**. The copy opens in Rehearsal with fresh codes and unassigned characters. Invite your practice players into that copy; memberships and invitations do not carry over.
5. Use **Invite players** or **Invite people**, create a Player invitation, and copy its code while displayed. Send the code yourself. ORACLE does not email invitations or redisplay a created code. Under **Characters**, assign one prewritten character to each joined player.
6. Ask each player to select **Open adventure**, choose their character, and scan or type the first RELIC prop code. **Read the markings** unlocks a message; the message's separate release word comes from the story. Its clue and the markings lead to the CIPHERBOX answer.
7. The correct answer opens the final WAYFINDER gathering. Three wrong answers open the authored alternative ending. Players join the appropriate scene and play the agreement in person. Attendance does not automatically fulfill a promise.
8. When the practice is complete, return to the original event and prepare its own memberships and character assignments. Use **Start rehearsal**, check the final setup, then **Go live**. Changing a rehearsal copy never changes its source event.

The starters also include draft rumors/news, a supply shop and fictional resource, published cooperative/fictional instruments, and a planning encounter. Review these before using them. Character spendable balances start at zero: grant resources explicitly under **Manage shops & balances**. Rule defaults alone grant no money. Starter operational encounters begin unlinked so they do not take over the original adventure's scene admission.

## Prepare your own event and characters

For an original story, expand **Other ways to start** and choose **Create a blank event**. Work through **World → Event → Material & rules → Review**. Select the theme and instruments, enter the public briefing, add entries with deliberate reader permissions, then choose **Create event**. Later use **Edit field kit** and **Save field kit**. Only the final save persists the setup; unsaved form text remains in that browser page.

Event name, description, location, theme terms, and rules are shared with members. Put private briefing material in entries marked **Organizer only**, and puzzle solutions in the private adventure fields. **Player preview** and **Prop display** help check the presentation. They do not reduce your account's privileges; use a separate player account on an unattended prop device.

Under **Characters**, expand **Character settings & factions**. Defaults allow players to create one active character and require approval. You can disable player creation, change the active-character limit, choose public badge fields, and define factions. Review submitted sheets with **Approve character** or **Request changes**. A requested change includes private feedback. For a prewritten sheet, choose **Unassigned · prewritten character** when creating it, then **Assign character** when its player has joined.

The first approval initializes starting inventory once. Reapproval does not refill it. Use the separate inventory controls with a recorded reason for corrections. Reassigning a sheet removes the former player's private access; it does not hand their private investigations, agreements, or captured receipts to the new player.

Player invitations can admit a group; Staff and Organizer invitations admit one person. The event owner controls role changes and Organizer invitations. Staff can perform their permitted tasks but cannot publish story material or manage every encounter by default. Assign staff to the encounters they will operate.

## Choose instruments for the story

All twelve are optional. Find the main adventure through **Open adventure**; expand **More tools for this event** and **Organizer and staff tools** for specialist workspaces. Briefing is an additional optional reading tool.

| Instrument | Prepare it | What happens during play |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RELIC | In **Prepare adventure**, write examinations and their required discoveries, skills, or flags; print its label. | A permitted examination adds its reading to the character's journal. |
| DEAD DROP | Write the message and any separate release word; include a transcript for optional uploaded audio. | An eligible character opens the message using the authored release word. The printed prop code only finds it. |
| CIPHERBOX | Set the answer, hints, maximum attempts, success result, and exhausted-attempt result. | Players submit answers and request available hints. Prepare a playable failure route. |
| WAYFINDER | Set a scene's location, conditions, time window, availability, and capacity. | Eligible characters join while space is available, or follow STAGEHAND admission when linked. |
| TRACE | Explain which observations should remain private and how players may intentionally share them. | Players create evidence, people, places, and theories, with permitted journal citations and links. Managers cannot bypass private notebooks. |
| WHISPER | Use **Prepare rumors & news** to write audience variants, sources, conditions, and private truth; publish the intended version. | Eligible characters collect attributed, unverified rumors. Publication does not make a claim true. |
| BROADSIDE | Review submitted bulletins and explicitly **Publish**. Add a correction note when republishing. | Players read approved news. A saved correction remains a draft until published; **Withdraw** removes current availability. |
| BAZAAR | Under **Manage shops & balances**, define fictional resources and finite stock, then grant character balances with a reason. | Purchases and mutually confirmed barter update stock, balances, inventory, and receipts together. |
| OATHBOOK | Explain the terms and witness roles; use **Review agreements** for disputes and reasoned rulings. | Participants accept the current revision and separately **Confirm fulfillment**. Witnesses cannot authorize someone else's payment. |
| SIGIL | Use **Prepare cooperative challenges** to publish roles, ordered checkpoints, time limits, components, and outcomes. | People cooperate around one host device. Only the host's reviewed components can be consumed on success. |
| STATIC | Use **Prepare fictional readings** to publish states, conditions, and result text. | Players read a clearly fictional prop/zone state and may collect a historical journal copy. Staff selections need a reason. |
| STAGEHAND | Use **Run encounters** to link a scene, assign staff, set readiness checks, capacity, and return window. | Staff open a ready encounter and dispatch accepted whole parties, then acknowledge their return. |

In **Sharing rules**, choose which readings can be offered in exchanges. New authored adventure instruments default to Restricted; complete starters make their RELIC and DEAD DROP readings Shareable. Organizer only also blocks new player discovery. Changing a policy clears pending exchange confirmations, but cannot retract previously authorized journal copies.

## Run encounters and pause play

In **Run encounters**, save the linked WAYFINDER scene, public message, private staff notes, staff assignments, capacity, return window, and readiness checklist. Saving a configuration clears readiness and pauses an open encounter. Assigned staff acknowledge preparation, then explicitly open it.

Create a waiting party or allow players to queue themselves. Every member must accept their current assignment, including a self-queued player. Queueing reserves no seats. Dispatch succeeds only for the whole eligible, accepted party within capacity. The server's return time is absolute; an overdue party still holds seats until staff record return or cancellation. Redirecting a waiting party clears its consent, so every member must accept again.

For an availability notice, staff submit public text and an organizer publishes it through BROADSIDE. An encounter change hides its older linked notice; prepare and approve a replacement. Staff notes never become the announcement automatically.

Use **Pause event** for a live pause and **Resume event** to reopen play. SIGIL attempts remain paused until explicitly resumed after checking server state. STAGEHAND return/cancel remains available during a pause; a pause does not extend its return deadlines. Connection loss never establishes a local challenge success, completed trade, or reserved scene place.

## Rehearse, reset, and close safely

**Reset rehearsal progress** exists only for a dedicated copy created with **Make rehearsal copy**, while that copy is in Rehearsal. A normal event merely placed in Rehearsal is not resettable. Live, Paused, Ended, and Archived events cannot be reset through this action. Check the event name and the **Dedicated rehearsal copy** banner before confirming.

A reset deletes that copy's discoveries, attempts, journals, attendance, exchanges/contacts/receipts, collected rumors, player investigations, balances, agreements, cooperative outcomes, fictional overrides, and operational runtime history. It restores captured starting inventory and authored initial shop stock. Authored definitions, character records, sharing policies, ordinary story publications/groups, and encounter configuration remain; linked operational announcements are cleared with operations. The source event is untouched. Reset is destructive within the copy; it is not undo and does not restore a live event.

Rehearsal copies support up to 100 source character profiles. They carry authored material with fresh identities/codes and no player progress, memberships, invitations, transactions, staff assignments, or parties. Reinvite players, assign characters/staff, grant practice balances, and complete readiness again. Browser notes or printouts from an earlier rehearsal are historical and must not be treated as fresh play.

To finish a live event, choose **End event** and confirm. Enrollment and live actions close; unfinished challenges and waiting/dispatched parties settle as canceled under their rules. Existing members retain permitted history. **Archive event** then makes it read-only; the lifecycle offers no reopen action after ending. There is no global sample-data reset. Retain or archive the practice event separately from the live event.

## Prepare for a real field event

Ask players to register **and sign in** before arrival, or stagger arrivals: the unchanged limit is 60 combined registration/login attempts per source IP per 15 minutes. Venue Wi-Fi may put everyone behind one address.

Open ORACLE and each player's journal while connected. Field desk notes need **Save note on this device**; other unsaved forms are not durable offline drafts. Use **Prepare paper fallback aids** for a freshly authorized player aid or minimal organizer list. Paper is timestamped history and cannot reserve capacity or authorize transfers.

Use the [pilot checklist](PILOT.md) with actual phones and participants before relying on ORACLE for an event. Its 58 human/device observations remain separate from automated tests, and Railway/event-network capacity has not been measured. For content reuse, see [themes and event packs](THEME_AND_EVENT_PACKS.md); a briefing pack cannot recover event progress. Operators should follow [deployment and recovery](DEPLOYMENT.md) for database protection.
